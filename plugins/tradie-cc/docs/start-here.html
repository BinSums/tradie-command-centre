Start here

Setting up your Command Centre

You do not need to be technical for this and you will not be typing anything complicated. Set aside about an hour. You can stop after any step and pick it up later.

What you will have at the end

One screen, every morning

A private web page you sign into with your own password, on your computer or your phone. Every weekday morning it reads your Xero and tells you the one thing worth doing: work you finished but never invoiced, an invoice that just tipped overdue, a quote going cold.

It all runs in accounts that belong to you. Nobody else can see your figures, including the person who gave you this.

Step 1About 10 minutes

Get Claude on your computer

Go to claude.com/pricing and take out a Pro subscription. Then download the app from claude.com/download. It works on Mac and Windows.

Install it the way you would any program: on a Mac, drag it into Applications. On Windows, run the file it downloads and click through. Then open it and sign in with the account you just paid for.

What you should see

Once it opens, there is a tab along the top called Code. Click it. That is where everything below happens. You are looking at a box you can type into.

Step 2About 5 minutes

Install one free program

It is called Node.js. You will never open it or look at it again, but the setup needs it to build your dashboard.

Go to nodejs.org/en/download and click the big green LTS button. It picks the right version for your computer on its own. Run the file it downloads and click Next through the installer, accepting whatever it suggests.

Do not skip this bit

Quit Claude completely, then open it again

A program installed while Claude was already running is invisible to it until you restart. On a Mac that means Claude, then Quit Claude, not just closing the window. This is the single most common thing that goes wrong, and it looks like the setup is broken when it is not.

Step 3About 3 minutes

Connect Xero

This is how it reads your figures. You are giving Claude permission to look at your Xero, and you can take that permission away again whenever you like.

  • Open Settings in Claude
  • Go to Connectors
  • Find Xero and click Connect
  • Sign in to Xero and approve it
What you should see

Xero showing as connected in that list. If it asks which organisation, pick your business.

Step 4About 2 minutes

Type three lines

Click into the Code tab. Type each line below and press Enter, one at a time, waiting for each to finish. Copy and paste them if that is easier.

Type these exactly
  1. /plugin marketplace add BinSums/tradie-command-centre
  2. /plugin install tradie-cc@sims-business-tools
  3. /tradie-cc:setup

The first two take a few seconds each. The third one starts talking to you.

What you should see

After the third line, Claude starts asking you questions. From here you are just having a conversation. It does the work and tells you what it is doing as it goes.

Step 5About 25 minutes

Answer its questions

It will ask you for four things, then get on with it. There is nothing to work out in advance except the password.

Your business nameWhatever should sit at the top of the dashboard.
Your timezone and currencyIt will suggest the right ones. You can just say yes.
A password for the dashboardPick one now. Nobody else ever sees it, not Claude and not the person who gave you this.

Along the way it will ask you to sign up for a free Cloudflare account, which is where your dashboard and your figures will live. It walks you through that when it gets there.

Near the end it spends ten minutes asking about your business: your busy season, how long a normal job takes you, your payment terms, who is always slow to pay. Do not rush that part. It is what stops the advice sounding like it came off the internet.

How you know it worked

Before it finishes, it runs your first morning report and reads two real numbers off it, then asks you to confirm they are right. If those numbers are yours, everything is connected properly.

From then on

What happens next

Every weekday morning a new card appears on your dashboard. Open it on your phone with your coffee. It takes thirty seconds to read and some mornings it will just tell you nothing needs you, which is a real answer and worth knowing.

There is also an Ask tab. Type a question in normal words and it answers from your own figures: "who owes us the most right now?", "how is cash tracking against last week?". It will tell you when it does not know something rather than making a number up, and it costs nothing to use.

Anything you want it to keep an eye on, or anything it could not know, just say so in normal words in the Code tab. Something like "stop chasing Blackwood, they are on a payment plan". It picks that up on the next run. You can also ask for whole new reports and tabs the same way, which is covered in the manual.

To put the dashboard on your phone: open the address in your phone browser, then the share menu, then Add to Home Screen. It behaves like an app after that.

The one job that stays yours

Export from Tradify once a week

Tradify has no way to hand its information over automatically, so once a week somebody exports a file and saves it into a folder. It takes about a minute and the setup shows you exactly how.

If it stops, your cash and invoice figures carry on perfectly, because those come from Xero. It is the jobs and quotes numbers that quietly go out of date. Every card tells you how old that file is, so you will see it happening. Put a weekly reminder in your phone.

If something goes wrong

Two things to try

While you are setting up

Just say what happened, in normal words, in the same box you have been typing in. "It says node is not recognised" or "the Xero screen did not appear" is plenty. It will work out what to do. You cannot break anything by saying the wrong thing.

Later, if a report does not show up or a number looks wrong

Open the Code tab and type this:

  1. /tradie-cc:check

It checks everything, fixes what it can on its own, and tells you in plain words if anything needs you.

One thing worth knowing: the morning reports run on this computer, so it needs to be on and Claude needs to be open around 7am. If it was asleep, the report runs the next time you open it. If this is a laptop that goes home with you, consider running it on the office computer instead.
