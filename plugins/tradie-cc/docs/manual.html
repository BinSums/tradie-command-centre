01

What this is

Your numbers already exist. Xero knows what you are owed, Tradify knows what work is booked, and your quoting spreadsheet knows what is out the door. The problem is not that the information is missing. It is that checking three systems is a job nobody does on a Tuesday when there are four crews to get out.

This puts one screen in front of that. Every weekday morning it reads across all of it and posts a single card saying what actually changed and what is worth doing about it. You read it on your phone before the first job, in about thirty seconds.

The distinction that matters

It is not a reporting tool

Xero and Tradify both have reports. Nobody opens them, because a report is a thing you have to remember to go and look at, and it hands you a list rather than a decision. This works the other way around: it comes to you, and its job is to be short.

A morning where nothing needs you says so in one line. That is deliberate. A system that manufactures urgency to justify itself gets ignored inside a month, and then the one morning it really matters, it gets ignored too.

The three things it is actually looking for

Underneath the routines, it is hunting the same small set of problems that quietly cost trades businesses money:

  • Work you finished but never invoiced. The single most common leak, and the hardest to spot, because it is invisible in Xero (no invoice exists) and looks finished in Tradify (the job is closed).
  • Quotes going cold. Conversion falls off a cliff after about a fortnight. A quote nobody followed up is not a maybe, it is a no that has not been written down yet.
  • The one invoice that matters. Not the ageing list. Trades businesses get hurt by a single large unpaid job, not by a tail of small ones, and a list sorted by age buries the big one underneath four old $400 invoices.

03

The routines

Each one runs on a schedule you set, does its work, and posts a card. You can turn any of them off. The advice is to start with the morning brief alone for a fortnight and add the others once you are actually reading it.

Weekdays, 7am

Morning brief

One card, thirty seconds, built to be read standing up. It leads with whatever actually changed since yesterday, in this order of priority: work finished but not invoiced, a large invoice that just tipped overdue, cash that will not cover the next fortnight of known bills, and quotes going cold.

If none of those apply it says the morning is quiet, and stops. It also states how old the Tradify data is on every single card, because that export is manual and goes stale without telling anyone.

Mondays, 8am

Cash and debtors

Not a list of overdue invoices. The two or three genuinely worth a phone call this week, chosen by what is at risk rather than by what looks worst on an ageing report.

It tracks debtor days, which is what you are owed divided by your average daily sales, and reports the trend rather than the level. Forty-seven days means nothing on its own. Forty-seven, up from thirty-four, is a story. It also checks what you owe before telling you that you are owed a lot, so it will not send you chasing $14,000 while a bigger bill is due Friday.

Where an invoice has sat a long time with no follow-up recorded, it says it is worth checking whether the job is in dispute before you ring, rather than telling you to ring. It cannot know that from Xero, and it does not pretend to.

Mondays, 8.30am

Jobs and quotes

Reads your latest export and compares it against the one before, because the interesting finding is almost always the difference between two weeks rather than the state of one.

  • Finished but not invoiced. Closed in Tradify, no matching invoice in Xero. First on the card, every time.
  • Quotes going cold. Sent more than fourteen days ago, not accepted, not rejected, no follow-up. Listed by value.
  • Jobs stalled. In progress and unmoved since last week, judged against how long that kind of job normally takes you, worked out from your own history rather than from an assumed number.
  • The pipeline. Quoted and undecided against work booked, which is the number that tells you whether next month is full.
Nightly, 4.41am

Routine audit

The one you never look at, and the one that keeps the others honest. The routines run without anybody watching, which means when one of them quietly fails to do a step, nothing says so. The card still appears. The number is just missing.

This checks every run from the night before for exactly that, repairs what it can on the spot, and tells you about anything it cannot fix itself. It also notices when the Tradify export has gone quiet and when a routine has stopped running altogether.

Leave this one switched on even if you turn the others off. Without it the failure mode is not a dashboard that breaks, it is a dashboard that looks fine and is quietly wrong.

04

Where the numbers come from

Three sources, and they are not equal. Understanding which is which is the difference between trusting the dashboard correctly and trusting it blindly.

SourceHow it arrivesWhat it gives
XeroLive connection, always current Cash at bank, invoices raised and paid, what you are owed and how old it is, what you owe, and the profit and loss.
TradifyCSV you export and upload Jobs and their status, quotes and their value, timesheets. As current as your last upload and no more.
Your quoting spreadsheetRead from a folder Quotes that never made it into Tradify, which are the ones most likely to go cold because nothing is tracking them. Uploaded the same way.
The honest limitation

Tradify cannot hand its data over automatically

Tradify has no public API, no webhooks and no Zapier connector. Its own integration list is accounting and payments: Xero, QuickBooks, MYOB, Sage, Stripe and Google Calendar. There is no supported way for an outside system to read your jobs and quotes on a schedule, and no amount of building changes that.

So anything financial reaches the dashboard through Xero, live, because Tradify pushes your invoices there already. Anything operational reaches it as a CSV that somebody in the office exports and drops in a folder. That takes about a minute and section 07 covers exactly how.

The risk is not that it breaks. It is that it goes stale quietly. If the export stops, the jobs numbers do not go blank, they keep showing last fortnight's figures, which is the dangerous way for a number to be wrong. So every card states the export's age, and once it passes seven days the routine stops presenting those figures as current and asks for a fresh one instead. Cash, invoices and debtors carry on regardless, because they come from Xero.

Why the dashboard says "no data" instead of zero

A number it could not find is stored as nothing, never as zero. Zero means your business genuinely had zero that day. If a source was unavailable, the card says which one and marks itself amber rather than quietly reporting on half the picture. This matters more than it sounds: a dashboard that charts a missing number as zero is how a good week starts looking like a disaster, and how you stop believing it.

06

Setting it up

You do not follow these steps by hand. You install the plugin, type one command, and Claude walks the sequence with you, asking four questions along the way. It takes about twenty minutes. The steps are written out here so you can see what it is doing rather than watching it happen blind.

/plugin marketplace add BinSums/tradie-command-centre
/plugin install tradie-cc@sims-business-tools
/tradie-cc:setup

Plugin documentation, if you want to read about the mechanism itself: docs.claude.com/en/docs/claude-code/plugins

Connect Xero

Open Settings, then Connectors, find Xero, click Connect, sign in to your Xero organisation and approve. This is the only step nobody else can do for you, and the setup will check it worked before going any further.

Answer four questions

Your business name as it should appear at the top of the dashboard, your timezone, your currency, and a password. Pick the password yourself and have it ready to type. It is never echoed back into the conversation.

It creates your database

A database is created inside your own Cloudflare account and the tables are built in it. Empty, ready, and yours.

npx wrangler d1 create your-business-cc-db
npx wrangler d1 execute your-business-cc-db --remote --file=schema.sql

It sets three passwords

Your dashboard password, plus two long random strings it generates itself: one that signs your login so a session cannot be forged, and one that lets the routines post their results in. Each is typed into a prompt rather than into a command, so none of them ends up saved in your command history.

It deploys the dashboard

You get a web address. Open it, sign in, and it will be empty. That is correct, because nothing has run yet. Then it posts a test card and asks you to refresh, which proves the whole loop works before anyone walks away from it.

It sets the permissions

So a 7am run never stops to ask permission for something nobody is awake to approve. It adds a specific list of things the routines are allowed to do, shows you that list first, and backs up your settings before changing anything. It does not remove anything you already had.

You fill in ten minutes about the business

Busy season, how long a typical job takes you, your payment terms, who is always slow to pay, and anything a routine should never chase. This is the boring step and it is the one that decides whether the advice fits your business or reads like it came off the internet.

It schedules the routines

Pick which ones and at what time. Start with the morning brief plus the nightly audit, and add the other two after a fortnight. One habit that sticks beats three that get ignored.

It runs one while you watch

Nothing finishes on a promise. The morning brief runs there and then, you look at the card together, and you check the Xero figures on it are really yours. Anything wrong gets fixed while somebody is still sitting in front of it.

If you want to understand the tools underneath: Wrangler commands and Cloudflare D1.

07

The export habit

This is the one part that depends on a person remembering. It takes about a minute, once a week, and the whole jobs and quotes half of the dashboard rests on it. Give it to whoever does the invoicing, not to whoever is on the tools.

Then drag the file onto Add an export on your dashboard's Home tab. It works out which export it is on its own and tells you how many rows it saved. You do not need to rename anything, and it does not matter which computer you do it from.

Jobs

Go to the Jobs page. Filter to what you want, then either tick the specific jobs or tick none, which exports everything in the tab you are on. Click Options in the top right, then Export Jobs to File.

help.tradifyhq.com › How To Export Jobs

Quotes

Go to the Quotes page and tick the quotes you want, or tick none to get everything in the current tab. Then click the Export icon. Note this one has no Options menu, which catches people out after they have learned the Jobs path.

help.tradifyhq.com › Export Quotes

Better, if you will do it: the Job Financial Report

Go to the Reports page and choose Job Financial Report. Set the date range, then click the arrow next to the download button and choose Download CSV File.

This one carries job value and cost, which the plain Jobs export does not. If it is present the routine prefers it for anything involving money, and says on the card which export a figure came from.

help.tradifyhq.com › Download Reports and Exports in a CSV file type

What happens if you stop

It tells you, rather than pretending

The Home tab lists every export with its age beside it, in green, then amber, then red as it gets older. Every card also carries a line like "Jobs data from the 25 Aug upload, 3 days old". Past seven days the routine turns amber, stops reporting those figures as current, and asks for a fresh one. It will not quietly present three week old job numbers as this morning's.

Cash, invoices, debtors and the profit and loss are unaffected, because Xero is live.

Tradify's own overview of getting your data out, including deleting it: Exporting or deleting your data from Tradify. Their Xero integration, which is what makes the live half work: tradifyhq.com/xero-and-tradify-addon.

08

Using it day to day

Reading a card

Tap any card on the Reports tab and the full report opens underneath the summary. The card is the headline; the report is everything the routine actually worked out, including the parts that did not make the cut.

The shared list

Routines add to it, you and the office tick things off, and it is the same list on every device. Adding the same job twice is safe: it recognises a job already on the list and leaves the original alone rather than stacking a duplicate.

Asking it for things, in plain English

Anything you want it to notice, say to Claude in normal words. It goes on a queue, and the next scheduled run picks it up before it does anything else, acts on it, and tells you on the card what it did.

  • "Stop chasing Blackwood, they are on a payment plan." It remembers, and stops putting them on the list.
  • "Tell me when quoted work drops under $100k." It watches for it.
  • "Add a job to book the excavator for the week of the 8th." Straight onto the shared list.

If it cannot do something, it says so on the card rather than dropping it silently.

Getting it onto a phone

Open the dashboard address in the phone browser, then the share menu, then Add to Home Screen. It opens without browser chrome after that and behaves like an app. Everyone who needs it uses the same password, so decide early whether the crew get it or only the office.

When something looks wrong

What you seeWhat it means and what to do
"Setup incomplete: DASH_PASSWORD is not set" The dashboard deployed before its passwords were set. Run npx wrangler secret put DASH_PASSWORD and the same for COOKIE_SECRET and INGEST_SECRET. It refuses to run half-configured on purpose rather than starting up unprotected.
Cards stopped appearing The routines are not reaching it. Usually the token in your local config no longer matches the deployed one, which happens if the secret was set twice. Set it again in both places rather than guessing which is right.
Jobs numbers look stale They are, and the card will say by how many days. Do a fresh export. If the card is not saying it, that is a bug worth reporting.
A card is amber saying Xero was unavailable The connection dropped or the authorisation expired. Reconnect Xero in Settings, then Connectors. The run deliberately reports the gap rather than filling it in.
A number looks wrong Open the full report behind the card. Every figure is traceable to the export or the Xero record it came from. If it is genuinely wrong, tell Claude in plain English and the correction is applied on the next run.
Nothing has run at all The schedule is not firing, most often because the computer was asleep. Check the scheduled tasks and confirm the timezone matches what you gave during setup.

How it is built

This section is for whoever is technical. Nobody needs to read it to use the thing.

The shape

A single Cloudflare Worker serving a dashboard, backed by a D1 database, both deployed into the client's own Cloudflare account. Routines run as scheduled Claude Code tasks on the client's machine, do their work through the Xero connector and the local exports, and POST their results in over HTTPS with a bearer token. The Worker holds no credentials for any third party system: Xero is reached by the routine, never by the dashboard.

What is stored

TableHolds
runsEvery routine run, append-only. Nothing is ever overwritten, so history is a permanent record rather than a rolling window.
metricsOne row per date and key, upserted, so re-running a day corrects rather than duplicates. Null means no data, never zero.
metric_metaThe label, unit and direction for each key, so a new number can be added without a code change.
notesDated recommendations, replaced wholesale per date and area so a re-run corrects its own advice.
todosThe shared list. Deduped on the open title.
assistant_queuePlain English requests waiting for the next run to pick them up.
pagesFull HTML reports published by a routine, which appear as their own tab.
importsUploaded CSVs kept whole and dated, so a routine can diff this week against last and any figure can be traced back. Storing them here rather than on a laptop is what lets the reports run in the cloud.
kbBusiness context, so the assistant knows the things that are not in the numbers.

Posting in

Everything goes through one helper, which loads the address and token itself so no secret is ever typed on a command line or left in shell history.

cc.sh post  /ingest          card.json     a run card
cc.sh postj /ingest-metrics  '{...}'       daily numbers
cc.sh postj /ingest-notes    '{...}'       recommendations
cc.sh post  /ingest-page     page.json     a full report as a tab
cc.sh postj /api/todo        '{...}'       add to the shared list
cc.sh get   /api/assistant-queue           what has been asked for

Security decisions worth knowing

  • Two separate credentials. A browser session cookie for people, a bearer token for routines. Ingest endpoints never accept the cookie, so a malicious page cannot post fake cards using a logged-in browser.
  • Sessions are signed, not stored. Signed with a secret that lives only in Cloudflare, compared in constant time so the comparison cannot be timed to guess it.
  • Published reports run sandboxed. Routine-authored HTML renders in a frame with scripts disabled and no access to the session, so a report cannot reach anything.
  • It fails closed. Missing any of the three secrets, the Worker refuses every request with a message naming the one that is missing, rather than starting up unprotected.

How it runs unattended

The routines run under a permission mode that does not prompt, paired with an explicit allowlist of what they may do. Deleting files, pushing code and sending mail are all deliberately left off it and still prompt.

The cost of not prompting is that a call outside the allowlist is refused silently: the run continues and the card still posts, with a gap where the data should be. That is the single failure mode of this design, and it is why the nightly audit exists and why it reads the previous runs rather than trusting them. Findings are grouped by which routine lost the call, because a count with no owner never gets fixed.

Deliberately not multi-tenant

One deployment per business

Every client gets their own Worker and their own database. There is no shared instance and no tenant column anywhere, which means there is no query that can accidentally return one business's data to another. It costs a few dollars a month per client instead of nothing, and it removes an entire category of failure.

11

Asking it questions

There is an Ask tab on the dashboard. Type a question in normal words and it answers from your own figures. It is there for the things the reports were never written to cover, and for when you are standing on a job and do not want to open anything else.

Things worth asking

  • Who owes us the most right now?
  • How is cash tracking against last week?
  • What should I chase today?
  • Are we quoting more or less than a month ago?
  • How old is my jobs data?

What it will not do

  • Make a number up. If it does not have something it says so.
  • Answer about anything outside your Xero and your uploads.
  • Actually do a job. Ask it to chase an invoice and it will tell you to put it on the list, not pretend it rang anyone.
Worth knowing

It answers from the same figures the cards are built from

It is not searching your accounts live. It reads the same summary the morning report reads, so the Ask tab and the cards can never disagree with each other. If a number is not on the dashboard, it will not be in the answer either, and it will tell you that rather than guessing.

It runs on the free AI included with your Cloudflare account, so asking costs nothing. If you ever want sharper answers, there is a paid upgrade in section 13.

12

Making it do more

The dashboard you were given is a starting point, not the finished thing. You add to it by asking, in the Code tab, in plain English. You are not writing anything and you cannot break it by asking badly.

The idea in one paragraph

Everything on the dashboard was put there by a routine

A routine is a job that runs on a schedule. It looks at your data, works something out, and sends it to the dashboard in one of five shapes: a card (a report), a number (a tile), a flag (something needing attention), a to-do, or a whole tab. Anything you want added is one of those five, and you get it by describing what you want.

Ask for a new report

Say when it should run and what it should tell you. Be specific about what you would actually do with the answer, because that is what makes it useful rather than just true.

Every Friday afternoon, check which jobs were finished this week
and whether each one has been invoiced yet. Put anything not
invoiced on my to-do list with the customer and the amount.
Each Monday, work out how long it takes us on average to invoice
a job after we finish it. Show it as a number on the dashboard and
tell me if it is getting worse.
Once a month, tell me if any single customer was more than 20%
of that month's revenue. That is the number that scares me.

Ask for a new number on the front page

Say what to count and which direction is good, so it colours correctly.

Add a tile for the total value of work booked but not yet started.
Higher is better.

Ask for a whole new tab

A tab is for detail you want to look through rather than be told.

Make a tab called Customers showing everyone we have worked for
this year, what they have spent, and when we last did a job for
them. Sort by spend.

Correct it, or tell it something it cannot know

This is the one most people never think to use, and it is the most valuable. The routines only know what is in Xero and your uploads. Everything else about your business has to be told to them once.

Stop chasing Blackwood. They are on a payment plan and paying
$2,000 a month.
December and January are dead for us. Do not flag a quiet month
then as a problem.
Retaining walls normally take us three weeks. Do not call one
stalled before that.

Say these in the Code tab any time. They go into a queue and the next run picks them up, acts on them, and tells you on the card what it changed.

Change or switch off something you already have

The morning brief is too early. Move it to 7:30.
Turn off the jobs and quotes report for now, we are not
uploading exports at the moment.
Two rules that keep it honest

Ask for decisions, not lists

"Show me all overdue invoices" gives you a list you will not read. "Tell me the two invoices most worth ringing about this week, and why those ones" gives you something you will act on. The whole system is built to be short, and a request for a big list works against it.

And whenever you add something that uses a number, say what it should do when it cannot find that number. The right answer is always to say so, never to guess. If you do not say it, say it anyway: it is worth the extra sentence.

If you break something

Nothing here is permanent

Reports can be switched off, tabs deleted, tiles removed. Your history is never overwritten, so an experiment cannot destroy last month's figures. If something looks wrong after a change, type /tradie-cc:check and it will find and fix what it can.

What it does not do

Worth saying plainly, so nobody discovers it in month two.

  • It does not write to Xero or Tradify. It reads. It will tell you to raise an invoice; it will not raise one. That is deliberate and I would push back on changing it.
  • It does not replace your bookkeeper. It surfaces what to ask them about.
  • It does not do scheduling or dispatch. Tradify does that and does it properly.
  • It does not fire the moment something happens. It runs on a schedule. An invoice that falls overdue at 2pm is on tomorrow's card, not an alert.
  • It cannot see anything Tradify will not export. Section 04 covers why.
